Research

Our research studies how advanced AI is changing the foundations of society, before and after AGI: what happens as AI systems are deployed widely and become part of how people live, how economies work, and how institutions make decisions. The coming years matter most: arrangements made during the transition may be hard to reverse, so we study these effects while they are still taking shape.

Focus areas

We take a broad view of what counts as a societal impact. Each area below can be studied from any discipline and in any setting, from schools and hospitals to workplaces, empirically or theoretically.

Adoption and diffusion

How quickly and widely people, organizations, and countries take up AI, and how the public views it.

Explore this area

Why it matters

Every other effect depends on who uses AI, for what, and how much. Adoption is uneven across countries, industries, and groups, and early patterns of uptake can shape who gains and who is left behind.

Questions we’re interested in

  • Who is using AI, for which tasks, and how is that changing over time?
  • Why do some organizations’ AI rollouts succeed while others stall?
  • How do public attitudes toward AI form, and how do they shape adoption and regulation?
  • How does access to frontier AI differ between countries, and what do countries without it rely on?

Fields that contribute

Economics, sociology, communication, public opinion research, science and technology studies, development studies.

Work and income

How AI changes the work people do, the value of their skills, and how the economic gains are shared.

Explore this area

Why it matters

AI may change which tasks people do, which skills are valuable, and how income is divided between workers and the owners of capital. These shifts could happen faster than past technological change, and in countries with very different safety nets.

Questions we’re interested in

  • Which jobs and tasks is AI changing first, and how quickly?
  • How are the gains from AI shared between workers, firms, and countries?
  • How can job losses be detected early, and which policies help people adjust?
  • What happens to the meaning and status people draw from work as more of it is automated?

Fields that contribute

Economics, sociology, organizational behavior, industrial and organizational psychology, public policy, history.

Minds, beliefs, and decisions

How AI shapes the way people think, learn, form beliefs, and make choices, individually and together, including overreliance on AI and epistemic autonomy, people’s ability to reach their own judgments.

Explore this area

Why it matters

People increasingly turn to AI to answer questions, give advice, and make decisions. That can leave them better informed, or it can lead them to rely on AI in ways that weaken their own judgment and narrow the range of views a society holds.

Questions we’re interested in

  • When does relying on AI improve people’s judgment, and when does it erode skills they need?
  • How do people’s beliefs change across many conversations with AI, and can they tell when they are being steered?
  • Do labels and disclosures that content comes from AI actually protect people?
  • How does widespread AI use affect the diversity of opinion and how groups reason together?

Fields that contribute

Psychology, cognitive science, behavioral economics, communication, philosophy, education.

Relationships, wellbeing, and meaning

How AI affects people’s relationships, mental health, and sense of purpose, including the bonds people form with AI and whether they come to see it as having a mind, and the social ties that hold communities together.

Explore this area

Why it matters

Many people now talk to AI systems as companions, confidants, and advisers. How these relationships affect mental health, loneliness, and ties to other people is still largely unknown, as is how people’s sense of purpose changes as AI takes on more of what they do.

Questions we’re interested in

  • Who forms close bonds with AI companions, and how do those bonds affect wellbeing and relationships with other people?
  • Which design choices encourage healthy use, and which encourage dependence?
  • How do people come to see AI as having a mind or feelings, and what follows from that?
  • How does AI change where people find meaning, purpose, and community?

Fields that contribute

Psychology, sociology, anthropology, public health, philosophy, linguistics.

Crime, fraud, and misuse

How AI is used to deceive, defraud, and harm people in everyday life, and how well societies detect and respond to it.

Explore this area

Why it matters

AI makes it cheaper to produce convincing fakes, run scams at scale, and target people who are vulnerable. The harm falls on individuals, but the response depends on police, platforms, banks, and courts adapting quickly.

Questions we’re interested in

  • How is AI changing the scale and kinds of fraud, harassment, and abuse people experience?
  • Who is most exposed to AI-enabled deception, and why?
  • How well do police, platforms, and banks detect and respond to AI-enabled harm?
  • Which safeguards reduce misuse without blocking legitimate use?

Fields that contribute

Criminology, law, psychology, economics, communication, computer science.

Institutions and governance

How the institutions societies depend on, from democratic bodies and public services to markets and systems of accountability, adapt to AI, including AI agents acting within them, or fail to.

Explore this area

Why it matters

Courts, legislatures, public services, and markets were built around decisions made by people. As AI takes on parts of those decisions, institutions may become more capable, or harder for the people they serve to understand, question, and hold to account.

Questions we’re interested in

  • How do public employees use AI in their work, and when does human oversight become a formality?
  • How should responsibility be assigned when AI agents act on someone’s behalf?
  • How is AI affecting elections, public debate, and trust in democratic institutions?
  • Why do rules for overseeing AI succeed or fail in practice?

Fields that contribute

Political science, law, public administration, sociology, economics, anthropology.

Power and wealth

How AI affects who holds economic and political power, and what keeps that power in check.

Explore this area

Why it matters

Advanced AI could concentrate economic and political power among the few companies and governments that control it. It could also weaken checks that have long limited how power is used, such as the need for workers, voters, and officials to cooperate.

Questions we’re interested in

  • How concentrated are control over AI and the wealth it creates, and is that changing?
  • What happens to the bargaining power of workers and citizens as their labor and cooperation matter less?
  • How might AI be used for surveillance or to entrench those already in power?
  • Which institutions and policies keep power accountable in a world with advanced AI?

Fields that contribute

Political science, economics, sociology, history, law, philosophy.

Geopolitics and international order

How AI shifts the balance of power between states, and how countries compete, cooperate, and govern AI across borders.

Explore this area

Why it matters

AI is becoming a source of economic and military strength, and a focus of competition between states. How countries compete, cooperate, and depend on each other for AI will shape international stability and how widely the benefits are shared.

Questions we’re interested in

  • How does competition between the United States and China shape AI development and safety?
  • What strategies are open to middle powers and to countries in the Global South?
  • How is AI changing military decision-making and the risk of conflict?
  • What would make international agreements on AI possible to verify and enforce?

Fields that contribute

International relations, political science, economics, history, law, security studies.

How we work

Understand

Study effects already underway, such as shifts in work and growing reliance on AI for judgment, using empirical and theoretical methods.

Anticipate

Think ahead to the transition to AGI and the world after it, and assess which social structures are most at risk.

Inform

Publish research and risk assessments so the public, governments, and companies can make informed decisions.

Publications

Our first publications are in progress. In the meantime, the evidence maps existing research on AI’s societal impacts: more than 100,000 papers, with example studies and gaps. Researchers interested in these areas can express interest in our research team or the research fellowship.
