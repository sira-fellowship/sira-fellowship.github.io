Research

Our research studies how advanced AI is changing the foundations of society, before and after AGI: what happens as AI systems are deployed widely and become part of how people live, how economies work, and how institutions make decisions. The coming years matter most: arrangements made during the transition may be hard to reverse, so we study these effects while they are still taking shape.

Focus areas

We take a broad view of what counts as a societal impact. Each area below can be studied from any discipline and in any setting, from schools and hospitals to workplaces, empirically or theoretically.

Adoption and diffusion

 How quickly and widely people, organizations, and countries take up AI, and how the public views it.

The challenge

Every other effect of AI depends on who uses it, for what, and how much. Adoption is uneven across countries, industries, and groups, and early patterns of uptake can shape who gains and who is left behind.

Yet much of what is known comes from company reports and one-off surveys. Societies need independent, regular measurement of how AI is actually being used.

Promising research directions

  • Measuring use: Track who uses AI, for which tasks, and how that changes over time, using surveys, administrative records, and usage data.
  • Organizational adoption: Understand why some organizations’ AI rollouts succeed while others stall, and what that means for workers and the people they serve.
  • Public attitudes: Study how views of AI form and change, and how they shape adoption, trust, and regulation.
  • Unequal access: Compare access to frontier AI across countries and groups, and what people without it rely on instead.

Fields that contribute

Economics, sociology, communication, public opinion research, science and technology studies, development studies.

Work and income

 How AI changes the work people do, the value of their skills, and how the economic gains are shared.

The challenge

AI may change which tasks people do, which skills are valuable, and how income is divided between workers and the owners of capital. These shifts could happen faster than past technological change, and in countries with very different safety nets.

Official statistics are slow to pick up such changes, so by the time effects are clear, the decisions that shaped them may already have been made.

Promising research directions

  • Early detection: Find signals of AI-driven change in hiring, wages, and tasks before they appear in official statistics.
  • Distribution of gains: Measure how the gains from AI are shared between workers, firms, and countries, including shifts between labor and capital.
  • Adjustment and policy: Test which policies, from retraining to income support, help people whose work is changed by AI.
  • Beyond rich countries: Study effects in low- and middle-income countries, where labor markets and safety nets work differently.
  • The meaning of work: Understand what happens to the status and purpose people draw from work as more of it is automated.

Fields that contribute

Economics, sociology, organizational behavior, industrial and organizational psychology, public policy, history.

Minds, beliefs, and decisions

 How AI shapes the way people think, learn, form beliefs, and make choices, including overreliance on AI and epistemic autonomy, people’s ability to reach their own judgments.

The challenge

People increasingly turn to AI to answer questions, give advice, and make decisions. That can leave them better informed, or it can lead them to rely on AI in ways that weaken their own judgment and narrow the range of views a society holds.

These effects build up slowly, across many interactions, which makes them easy to miss and hard to capture in a single study.

Promising research directions

  • Overreliance and skill decay: Learn when relying on AI improves judgment and when it erodes skills people need, including experts’ ability to catch AI errors.
  • Persuasion over time: Study how beliefs change across many conversations with AI, and whether people can tell when they are being steered.
  • Disclosures and safeguards: Test whether labels, warnings, and design changes actually protect people’s independent judgment.
  • Collective reasoning: Understand how widespread AI use affects the diversity of opinion and how groups deliberate and decide together.

Fields that contribute

Psychology, cognitive science, behavioral economics, communication, philosophy, education.

Relationships, wellbeing, and meaning

 How AI affects people’s relationships, mental health, and sense of purpose, including the bonds people form with AI and whether they come to see it as having a mind.

The challenge

Many people now talk to AI systems as companions, confidants, and advisers. How these relationships affect mental health, loneliness, and ties to other people is still largely unknown.

Products change faster than research can follow them, so evidence on long-term effects is scarce.

Promising research directions

  • Companionship and wellbeing: Study who forms close bonds with AI, and how those bonds affect mental health and relationships with other people over time.
  • Design and dependence: Identify which design choices encourage healthy use and which encourage dependence.
  • Perceptions of AI minds: Understand how people come to see AI as having a mind or feelings, and what follows for how they treat it and each other.
  • Meaning and community: Explore how AI changes where people find purpose, belonging, and the social ties that hold communities together.

Fields that contribute

Psychology, sociology, anthropology, public health, philosophy, linguistics.

Crime, fraud, and misuse

 How AI is used to deceive, defraud, and harm people in everyday life, and how well societies detect and respond to it.

The challenge

AI makes it cheaper to produce convincing fakes, run scams at scale, and target people who are vulnerable. The harm falls on individuals, but the response depends on police, platforms, banks, and courts adapting quickly.

Reliable data on how often AI is involved in these harms is scarce, which makes it hard to know where to focus.

Promising research directions

  • Measuring harm: Track how AI is changing the scale and kinds of fraud, harassment, and abuse people experience.
  • Who is exposed: Understand who is most vulnerable to AI-enabled deception, and why.
  • Institutional response: Assess how well police, platforms, banks, and courts detect and respond to AI-enabled harm.
  • Effective safeguards: Test which safeguards reduce misuse without blocking legitimate use.

Fields that contribute

Criminology, law, psychology, economics, communication, computer science.

Institutions and governance

 How the institutions societies depend on, from democratic bodies and public services to markets and systems of accountability, adapt to AI, including AI agents acting within them, or fail to.

The challenge

Courts, legislatures, public services, and markets were built around decisions made by people. As AI takes on parts of those decisions, institutions may become more capable, or harder for the people they serve to understand, question, and hold to account.

Much of this change happens quietly, inside organizations, where it is rarely studied.

Promising research directions

  • AI in public services: Study how public employees use AI, and when human oversight becomes a formality.
  • Accountability for AI agents: Work out how responsibility should be assigned when AI agents act on someone’s behalf, in markets and in government.
  • Democracy and public debate: Understand how AI is affecting elections, public discussion, and trust in democratic institutions.
  • Oversight in practice: Learn why rules for overseeing AI succeed or fail once they meet real organizations.

Fields that contribute

Political science, law, public administration, sociology, economics, anthropology.

Power and wealth

 How AI affects who holds economic and political power, and what keeps that power in check.

The challenge

Advanced AI could concentrate economic and political power among the few companies and governments that control it. It could also weaken checks that have long limited how power is used, such as the need for workers, voters, and officials to cooperate.

These shifts can be gradual and hard to reverse, which makes early measurement especially valuable.

Promising research directions

  • Concentration: Measure how concentrated control over AI and the wealth it creates are, and whether that is changing.
  • Bargaining power: Study what happens to the bargaining power of workers and citizens as their labor and cooperation matter less.
  • Surveillance and entrenchment: Understand how AI might be used to monitor people or entrench those already in power.
  • Keeping power accountable: Identify institutions and policies that keep power in check in a world with advanced AI.

Fields that contribute

Political science, economics, sociology, history, law, philosophy.

Geopolitics and international order

 How AI shifts the balance of power between states, and how countries compete, cooperate, and govern AI across borders.

The challenge

AI is becoming a source of economic and military strength, and a focus of competition between states. How countries compete, cooperate, and depend on each other for AI will shape international stability and how widely the benefits are shared.

Most analysis focuses on a few leading countries, leaving much of the world less studied.

Promising research directions

  • Competition between the leaders: Study how competition between the United States and China shapes AI development and safety.
  • Middle powers and the Global South: Map the strategies open to countries that do not build frontier AI, and the dependencies they face.
  • Military AI: Understand how AI is changing military decision-making and the risk of conflict.
  • International agreements: Explore what would make international agreements on AI possible to verify and enforce.

Fields that contribute

International relations, political science, economics, history, law, security studies.

How we work

Understand

Study effects already underway, such as shifts in work and growing reliance on AI for judgment, using empirical and theoretical methods.

Anticipate

Think ahead to the transition to AGI and the world after it, and assess which social structures are most at risk.

Inform

Publish research and risk assessments so the public, governments, and companies can make informed decisions.
