SIRA’s mission is to discover and publish evidence about the societal impacts of advanced AI systems, the associated risks, and the effectiveness of present and future interventions, so humanity can navigate the transition to AGI and thrive in the world that follows.

What we do

SIRA studies the societal impacts of advanced AI systems to understand whether society is prepared for the transition to AGI and the world that follows. We publish research on AI’s present and future effects and risk assessments so that the public, governments, and companies can make informed decisions. More about SIRA

What we study

How AI is changing the foundations of society: how people work and earn, how they form beliefs and make decisions, how institutions govern, and how power and wealth are distributed. We study effects already underway and project them forward to the transition to AGI and beyond, asking which social structures are likely to bend, which might break, and what would help them adapt.

Research fellowship

Our core program brings exceptional researchers from any discipline, from undergraduates to senior academics, into this work, pairing them with mentors who already study these questions.

In-house research

As SIRA grows, we are building an in-house research team to lead our assessments and pursue the questions we judge most urgent. Together with our fellows, they build an evidence base the public, governments, and companies can use to prepare for what comes next. Express interest here

FAQ

Is SIRA running yet?

Not yet. SIRA is a proposal, and we are currently gathering feedback, mentors, partners, and funding. We are targeting a first cohort in Summer 2027. You can read the full proposal and leave feedback in the comments.

What kind of research does SIRA support?

Empirical and theoretical research on how advanced AI affects people, institutions, and society, now and after AGI. That includes experiments, surveys, interviews, ethnography, and quantitative modeling, as well as formal models, conceptual analysis, and theory building. It treats AI as part of larger social and technical systems, which makes it distinct from technical alignment work on one side and from regulatory policy design on the other. The evidence page collects more than 250 examples.

How is this different from a policy or governance fellowship?

SIRA is a research organization, not a policy fellowship. Policy and governance research asks “what rules should govern this?”: it designs regulation, evaluates compliance, and advises decision-makers. SIRA asks “what is actually happening, and why?”: it studies how people and institutions reason about, trust, use, and are changed by AI in practice. The same topic can be approached either way:

  • PolicyWhat disclosure rules should apply to AI-generated political messages?Social scienceHow much do AI-written messages actually shift people’s views, and who is most persuadable?
  • PolicyWho should be liable when AI advice causes harm?Social scienceWhen do people over-trust AI advice, and what makes them check it?
  • PolicyHow should AI labs be required to report safety incidents?Social scienceHow do people inside AI labs decide what counts as an incident worth reporting?

There is a middle ground where the two meet, and social science findings often inform policy. But SIRA projects start from a social science question, whether answered with data or with theory, not from a regulation to design. Many fellowships already support policy and governance research; very few support this.

What if I work in a different field? Can I still apply?

Yes. We welcome applicants from any field. We expect most fellows to come from the social, behavioral, and brain sciences, such as psychology, cognitive science, neuroscience, economics, and sociology. If you are a researcher in another discipline, such as law, philosophy, history, public health, or computer science, and you have a strong question about AI’s effects on society, we encourage you to apply.

How is SIRA funded?
