Who should apply

Researchers who want to do rigorous empirical or theoretical work on how advanced AI is changing society.

We are looking for researchers at any career stage, from undergraduates to senior academics and mid-career professionals, who study how people and institutions reason about AI, trust it, use it, and are changed by it. We expect most fellows to come from the social, behavioral, and brain sciences, including psychology, cognitive science, neuroscience, economics, sociology, political science, communication, anthropology, and organizational behavior.

We welcome applicants from any field. If you are a researcher in another discipline, such as law, philosophy, history, public health, or computer science, and you have a strong question about AI’s effects on society, we encourage you to apply.

SIRA welcomes both empirical work (experiments, surveys, interviews, ethnography, and quantitative modeling) and theoretical work (formal models, conceptual analysis, and theory building). What we don’t do is regulatory policy design. For examples of this kind of work, browse our selected studies.

Two research streams

Fellows choose a main stream when they apply. Both streams cover effects already underway and the world after AGI.

AI safety

Harms that come from AI systems and how people use them

How AI persuades, misleads, and is misused, and when people over-trust it or stop checking its work. Projects ask where these harms arise, who is most exposed, and which safeguards actually reduce them.

Example topics: persuasion and manipulation, overreliance on AI advice, deception, misuse, human oversight

AI resilience

Whether society can absorb and adapt to advanced AI

How work, institutions, and the distribution of power change as AI grows more capable, and what helps them adapt. Projects ask which social structures are likely to bend or break, and what would keep them working.

Example topics: labor and income, democratic institutions, concentration of power and wealth, social trust, life after AGI

How it works

Twelve weeks of full-time research with a mentor, a research manager, and a cohort of ~ 20. Mentors are confirmed first (see who has joined so far).

What every fellow gets

  • Mentorship or advising from researchers in academia, industry, and government who study AI resilience and safety.
  • Research management. A dedicated research manager helps you scope a project you can finish in the program and keeps you on track.
  • Seminars and speakers covering AI resilience and safety, including technical AI safety, and the social sciences, so you get context beyond your mentor’s specialty.
  • An in-person cohort. Working alongside other fellows every day is a large part of what makes short programs like this work.
  • Financial support. A stipend, housing, most meals, travel, and research funds.
  • A closing symposium where you present your work to mentors and the wider AI resilience and AI safety communities.

Example research questions

These illustrate the kind of work fellows might take on, from harms already underway to the world after AGI. See the evidence for published studies on questions like these.

AI safety

  • PsychologyAs AI becomes the default aid for everyday judgment, how does that shift human epistemic autonomy and vulnerability to manipulation?
  • Cognitive scienceWhen people offload reasoning and memory to AI, do they lose the skills needed to check its answers, and under what conditions does that make human oversight fail?
  • NeuroscienceHow does relying on AI for memory, navigation, and decisions change the brain systems those skills depend on, and are the changes reversible?
  • CommunicationHow do AI-generated content and persuasion degrade the information ecosystem in ways that weaken society’s ability to coordinate a response to AI?

AI resilience

  • EconomicsIf AI can do most cognitive work, how will income, bargaining power, and the value of human skills shift, and which institutions could cushion the transition?
  • Political scienceHow might advanced AI concentrate economic and political power, and which institutional checks are most likely to hold as it does?
  • SociologyAs AI takes on more work and care roles, how do families and communities reorganize, and what keeps social ties and trust from eroding?

FAQ

Where will it take place?

We plan to run in person, either as a dedicated stream inside an existing AI safety fellowship or as a standalone program. Options include New York, Boston, San Francisco, London, and a remote or hybrid format.

Do I need a technical background?

No. We expect most fellows to come from the social, behavioral, and brain sciences, though we welcome applicants from any field. Seminars and mentorship give you the AI resilience and safety context you need, and research managers help you scope a project you can finish within the program.

Will projects involve human participants?

Some will, and we welcome them. Ethics review would most likely go through the mentor’s or fellow’s institution, so projects that need it should be scoped before the fellowship begins.

Is it paid?

Yes. We plan to provide a competitive living stipend for the length of the program, plus housing, most meals, and travel to and from the host city. Senior fellows may receive a higher stipend. Projects also draw on a shared research fund for compute, software, and data or survey access.
