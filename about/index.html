About SIRA

We study the societal impacts of advanced AI, before and after AGI.

  1. What we do

    SIRA is an organization that studies the societal impacts of advanced AI systems to understand whether society is prepared for the transition to AGI and the world that follows. We publish research on AI’s present and future effects and risk assessments so that the public, governments, and companies can make informed decisions.

    Our research measures how AI is changing the foundations of society: how people work and earn, how they form beliefs and make decisions, how institutions govern, and how power and wealth are distributed. We study effects already underway, such as shifts in labor markets and growing reliance on AI for judgment, and project them forward to the transition to AGI and beyond, asking which social structures are likely to bend, which might break, and what would help them adapt.

    Our core program is a research fellowship that brings exceptional researchers from any discipline, from undergraduates to senior academics, into this work, pairing them with mentors who already study these questions. As SIRA grows, we are building an in-house research team to lead our assessments and pursue the questions we judge most urgent. Together, they build an evidence base the public, governments, and companies can use to prepare for what comes next.

  2. Our mission

    SIRA’s mission is to discover and publish evidence about the societal impacts of advanced AI systems, the associated risks, and the effectiveness of present and future interventions, so humanity can navigate the transition to AGI and thrive in the world that follows.

    At some point, AI systems will probably be able to do most of what humans can do, including much of the work that sustains households, organizations, and governments. A transition like this could reshape how people earn a living, how they come to believe what they believe, how institutions make decisions, and who holds the power, faster than at any point in history. It could bring enormous prosperity, or deep and lasting harm.

    In particular, the institutions societies depend on (labor markets, democratic governance, shared sources of information, systems of accountability) could fail to adapt in time. Most people would not choose this outcome, but it could still happen gradually and unintentionally: effects may be spread too thinly to notice, no one may be responsible for tracking them, and by the time harms are obvious, the structures that would have absorbed them may already have weakened.

    Because these changes can unfold faster than institutions can study them, the world needs early, evidence-backed signals of how AI is changing society, rigorous tests of which interventions help, and serious thinking about the world after AGI before it arrives. That depends on far more researchers working on these questions, from every discipline, and on independent institutions that can study the evidence and say what it means. We hope to help provide both.
